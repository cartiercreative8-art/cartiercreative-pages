You can't outspend the big guys. You can't outstaff them. Their trucks are newer and their ads are everywhere.

But a homeowner with water on the basement floor isn't shopping for the biggest company. They want two things. Someone who picks up. Someone who shows up.

That's a race a small shop can win every time.

## Speed is the whole game

In 2011, researchers writing in [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads) looked at how fast companies answered new inquiries. Firms that reached out within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a real conversation with that buyer as firms that waited even one hour longer. Waiting a full day was far worse.

That study wasn't about plumbers. It didn't need to be. Anyone who has had a furnace quit in January knows how it goes. You call down the list. The first person who calls back gets the job.

## What you have that they don't

When a big company calls back, it's a dispatcher reading a script. When you call back, it's the person who will actually do the work. Customers feel that.

It's why reviews for small shops name the owner. "Mike came out the same day." "Dana explained everything before she started." Nobody writes that about a call center.

Your name is your brand. Fast and personal beats big and slow.

## Three habits that make speed automatic

1. **Never let a call die in voicemail.** If you can't pick up, a text should go out within a minute. "Hey, it's Mike. I'm on a job. What's going on?" That one text keeps the customer from dialing the next number.
2. **Call back at set times.** Before lunch and before dinner, every day. A short list and fast calls. Customers forgive a wait when they know you'll get back to them.
3. **Ask for the review in the driveway.** The customer is happiest the moment the job is done. Send the link before you back out. Reviews that mention speed sell the next job for you.

## The slow season is the setup

Winter slows a lot of trades down. Use it. Get a text-back running. Clean up your Google profile. Ask last season's customers for reviews.

When spring hits, you'll already be the fastest shop in town.

> You don't have to be the biggest. You have to be the one who answered.
