What a scraped list would have gotten wrong

Three failures in this set would have passed a normal verification pass. Each one changed a row in the list below.

01

Dead mailbox behind a catch-all domain

Patriot Growth Insurance Services

Patriot named a new COO on 27 October, eight days before this list. His mailbox does not exist yet. The domain answers as catch-all, so most verification tools pass any address on it through as accept-all and the send lands nowhere.

  • rjain@patriotgis.commailbox not found
  • tcameron@patriotgis.comvalid
  • mday@patriotgis.comvalid
  • gparker@patriotgis.comvalid

Three confirmed addresses on the same server resolve and his does not, so the house format is right and the box is genuinely unprovisioned. The row carries their VP of Operations instead.

03

Staff mail on a different domain from the website

PLS Logistics Services

Their public site is plslogistics.com. Staff mail routes somewhere else. Send to the public domain and every contact at the company fails in one pass, which also degrades the sending domain for everything after it.

  • plslogistics.compublic website
  • pghlogistics.comwhere staff mail actually routes

Verified separately from the address itself. This is why the mail domain is its own column in the file.

The queue

Every row opens to its evidence: how the contact was confirmed, what was ruled out, and where the judgment is soft.

    How each row was built

    1. Find the operations owner, not a generic contact or whoever sits highest on the org chart.
    2. Confirm the person still holds the seat. Three did not, and all three were replaced rather than dropped.
    3. Resolve the mail domain separately from the address, because a company's staff mail does not always run on its public website.
    4. Verify the mailbox itself, and treat a catch-all domain as unproven rather than valid.
    5. Rate the confidence and write down what would make it wrong.
